Fuel System

Rotary engines need a fuel system sized for the engine, injectors, and power goal.

What I did

I'll start at the pump and work my way back. When the build first started I replaced the old fuel pump with a new OEM one, replaced the sock, inspected the tank, and replaced the fuel filter. One of my rubber lines that goes from the filter to the hardline was dry rotted and needed to be replaced. Sadly autozone sold me EMI fuel line instead of EFI. EMI is for emmisions and low psi fuel flow while EFI is meant for higher pressures lke going from your tank to your injectors. EMI instantly blew open leaking fuel everywhere once the pressure built up. So remember when buying rubber lines make sure it says EFI. Moving to the engine bay Originally on the miata there are three fuel lines: feed, return, and a vent. I cut the vent line and pointed it away from the engine bay. I tried retaining as much of the OEM hardline as I could but bending alot without breaking was difficult so I cut it and routed the feed to my primary injectors then secondaries and finally the fuel pressure regulator prior to making the return.

At the beggining I didn't know much about injector sizes mattered in rotaries and assumed the bigger the better. I made the mistake of going with 2200cc mustang injectors for all for of mine. Lol I only get 10 miles per gallon. My issue isn't running out of injectors, its being able to easily control those massive injectors. Imagine hooking up a fire hose to a water ballon, thats exactly what I did. In a rotary the primaries do most of the work from idle to low cruise to initial acceleration. The secondaries pick up the rest like mid cruise and up and flooring it.

What this section should cover

Fuel System Guide: These recommendations are intended as general starting points for staged-injection 13B rotary engines running gasoline. Actual injector and fuel pump requirements vary based on fuel type, fuel pressure, boost pressure, horsepower measurement, injector duty cycle, engine configuration, and fuel system design. Fuel pump flow should be verified at the engine's actual operating fuel pressure, as pump flow decreases as fuel pressure increases. E85 and other fuels may require greater injector and fuel pump capacity.
